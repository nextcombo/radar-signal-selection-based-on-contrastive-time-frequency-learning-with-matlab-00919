# Radar Signal Selection Based on Contrastive Time-frequency Learning [with MATLAB Code]

Summary
With the advancement of radar technology, the electromagnetic environment in which radar operates is becoming increasingly complex. Existing methods for radar signal classification and discrimination have high costs associated with data labeling and poor generalization capabilities. To address this issue, a radar signal recognition method based on time-frequency analysis and contrastive learning has been proposed. This approach improves the classification process without the need for labels by leveraging the characteristics of radar signals. The proposed time-frequency contrastive learning method can classify radar signals using unsupervised data, enhancing the generalization capability of the classification model, especially under low signal-to-noise ratio conditions. Experimental results demonstrate that this method exhibits excellent performance, achieving a classification accuracy rate of 98.17% for radar signals.
Introduction
Radar reconnaissance is an integral part of modern electronic countermeasures technology and the cornerstone of electronic defense. Radar signal classification is a crucial stage in radar reconnaissance. With the advancement of radar technology, the complexity of the electromagnetic environment continues to expand [1]. Effectively and efficiently classifying signals is a significant issue.
Traditional radar signal classification algorithms typically use pulse description words (PDW), which include carrier frequency (CF), pulse width (PW), pulse amplitude (PA), arrival time (TOA), and arrival direction (DOA) [3]. In addition to inter-pulse features, intra-pulse modulation features are also important manifestations of subtle features in radar signals [4]. Using intra-pulse features for signal classification and discrimination has become an important research area. However, single-domain or domain-based analysis methods cannot effectively describe the changing patterns and characteristics of signals. Time-frequency analysis can better reflect the energy density distribution of non-stationary signals. By mapping one-dimensional signals to two-dimensional time-frequency domains, time-frequency analysis can significantly enhance the robustness of algorithms and reduce noise impact.
The critical component of signal sorting is feature extraction. Deep learning has demonstrated impressive capabilities in feature extraction for computer vision and natural language processing, among other fields. Utilizing the knowledge of deep learning, such as reinforcement learning and transfer learning, to independently extract features from radar signals has been the subject of numerous studies. The application of deep learning theory in the field of radar signal processing is one of the most promising research areas [6].
The performance of deep learning models is often closely related to the quality of the dataset. Robust deep learning networks with good generalization capabilities are typically obtained through training on large labeled data sets. However, building a large radar dataset containing labeled labels is very difficult due to the high number of unknown radars in electronic warfare. Unsupervised data is easier to obtain than supervised data. To reduce dependence on labeled data, network training can be conducted using unsupervised data for signal classification. Therefore, it is worth exploring methods to mine unsupervised data features using heuristic tasks, such as predicting image rotation angles or puzzle-solving tasks [7]. For radar signal sorting, a suitable sorting task that matches the characteristics of radar signals is needed.
This paper proposes a radar signal sorting method based on time-frequency analysis and contrastive learning—Contrast Time-Frequency Learning (CTFL). The purpose of this method is to reduce dependence on labeled data and enhance the generalization capability of radar signal sorting methods. Based on Multi-Synchronous Compression Transform (MSST), the signal is transformed into a two-dimensional time-frequency image. The enhanced time-frequency image (TFI) is used to train a contrastive learning model to extract features from the signal. With the characteristics of radar signals, effective classification can be achieved.

The related work on radar signal classification based on time-frequency analysis and machine learning has been proposed. Choi-Williams distribution (CWD) is used for time-frequency analysis, followed by information theory to filter the potential features of the signals [8]. A multilayer perceptron is employed for classifying radar signals with a noise ratio of 6dB. To enhance the robustness and generalization capability of the classification system, transfer learning is applied to the selection of intra-pulse features of radar signals. Transfer learning is utilized to reconstruct the cube of energy, which represents the original radar signals [9]. Support vector machines are then used for feature classification, improving both accuracy and robustness. All these feature extraction methods require the rich experience of professionals. Although these feature extraction methods are semantically clear, their application is limited by the categories of signals. These features are not very universal, making the classification of new modulated signals generally unreliable. Moreover, the issue of overfitting cannot be overlooked. When the dimensionality of the features increases, the accuracy does not improve accordingly.
To make the classification method more universal, automatic feature extraction is needed. In recent years, deep learning has developed rapidly, showing excellent performance and strong generalization capabilities in fields such as computer vision and natural language processing. Deep learning brings many possibilities and innovations for automatically extracting features from signals. The introduction of Cohen's class time-frequency distribution kernel function has improved the classification of radar signal selection [10]. It performs well for low noise ratio signals. Besides CNN, random forest (RFF) is also used for classifying radar signals. Stacked sparse autoencoder (SSAE) is used for identifying low intercept probability radar signals [11]. CWD is used for time-frequency transformation, followed by RFF for recognizing the features extracted by SSAE. The accuracy of this method can reach 96.4% [12]. However, all these methods require a large training set to achieve high accuracy.
This paper proposes a deep learning-based radar signal classification method that can utilize unsupervised data instead of supervised data. Compared with existing methods, this method has better generalization capability and its feature representation module can effectively improve the accuracy of classification tasks under low noise ratio conditions.
